Abstract: With the widespread popularization of unmanned sweepers, path planning has been
recognized as a key component affecting their total work efficiency. Conventional path planning
methods often only aim to improve work efficiency while ignoring energy optimization, a crucial
factor for sustainable development. In this paper, an energy- and time-minimization unmanned
sweeper arc path problem (ETM-ARP) is investigated, and the effects of road slope, dynamic changes
in on-board mass, mode switching of vehicle work, and traffic lights are taken into consideration to
meet the requirements of a realistic structured road scenario. A new multi-objective mixed-integer
nonlinear planning model is proposed for this problem. To solve this model, we propose a deep Q-
network (DQN) and Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search Algorithm (ALNS)-driven non-dominated
sorting genetic algorithm II (QALNS-NSGA-II). The novelty of this algorithm lies in integrating DQN
into ALNS, to guide high-quality adaptive operator selection during the search process based on
additional information. The computational results of various examples confirm the effectiveness of
the proposed method. The proposed method can be used to improve the efficiency and sustainability
of unmanned sweepers for sweeping on structured roads.
Keywords: unmanned sweeper; path planning; multi-objective optimization; DQN
1. Introduction
With the rapid development of the economy, the capacity of urban and rural envi-
ronmental construction has been significantly improved, and the sanitation automobile
industry has gradually transformed to electrification and intelligence. Unmanned sweepers
have been widely promoted in recent years due to their advantages of high efficiency and
low cost [1], and are expected to become the main force of urban street cleaning in the
future. Route planning is the key to unmanned sweeper operation, directly affecting work
efficiency. Unmanned sweepers need to start from a designated location to cover a specific
neighborhood, during which they may need to stop and wait at signalized intersections
and pass quickly over repeated passing sections to improve efficiency. The core of the
solution to unmanned sweeper path planning lies in determining the most cost-effective
full-coverage routes, often optimizing travel distance, energy consumption, or time to
improve sweeping efficiency while satisfying constraints, which supports the sustainability
of smart cities.
For structured urban roads, traffic signals are common traffic control facilities, and
vehicles often encounter multiple signal-controlled intersections in the course of traveling,
which may cause interruptions to vehicle movement. At present, in the path planning
algorithms that consider the influence of traffic signals, scholars at home and abroad have
proposed algorithm models from different perspectives. Song et al. [2] proposed a path

the impact of different driving styles on energy consumption when vehicles pass through
signalized intersections. In fact, idling, braking, and acceleration behaviors caused by traffic
signals are the main contributors to time wastage and energy consumption [4]. Georgiadis
et al. [5] incorporated simulated environmental factors such as traffic lights and stop signs
in their model and accurately estimated the energy consumption of electric vehicles. The
results showed that the path planning model managed to reduce energy consumption by
about 13% although it increased traveling time by 6%.
In the application scenario of unmanned sweepers, the vehicle serves road seg-
ments rather than customer nodes on the road. This type of path planning problem
is known as the Arc Routing Problem (ARP). For the optimization of ARP problems,
different objective functions have been proposed in the literature, mainly including min-
imizing travel distance [6–8], minimizing travel time [9,10], minimizing total cost (e.g.,
vehicle configuration cost and operation cost, etc.) [11,12], and minimizing vehicle energy
consumption [13,14]. Route selection is a major factor influencing vehicle energy consump-
tion. However, some articles tend to overlook factors that affect the energy consumption of
electric vehicles [14,15], such as changes in load mass, speed, road slope, and regenerative
braking systems. Instead, they simplify the relationship between a vehicle’s energy con-
sumption and the distance traveled to a linear one. Boriboonsomsin et al. [16] showed that
there is a conflict between the shortest distance, shortest time, and most energy efficient
paths, where the most energy-efficient path reduces the energy consumption by 12% to
14%, but extends the travel time by 16% to 22% compared to the shortest-distance path.
Based on this, this study proposes a multi-objective unmanned sweeper arc path problem
(ETM-ARP) with the objective of minimizing energy consumption and travel time, aiming
to analyze and provide trade-offs between the two. The optimization of this problem is
important for improving the cleaning efficiency of unmanned sweepers and promoting
environmental sustainability.
The ARP was first proposed by Golden et al. [17] and has been proven to be an NP-
hard problem. Since ETM-ARP involves various constraints (e.g., signal light constraints,
full road coverage constraints) and nonlinearities (e.g., vehicle energy consumption), find-
ing an optimal solution in a reasonable time is a challenging task. Several approaches have
provided potential solutions in recent years, mainly including exact and metaheuristic algo-
rithms. The exact algorithms mainly include the cutting plane [11], the column generation
method [18], and branch-and-bound [19]. However, this kind of method makes it difficult
to obtain a good feasible solution for large-scale problems in a short time. Metaheuris-
tic algorithms include ant colony algorithms [20], genetic algorithms [21], the Adaptive
Large Neighborhood Search Algorithm [22], etc. However, heuristic algorithms suffer from
suboptimality, lack of convergence, and limited generalization when dealing with real-life
optimization problems.
Reinforcement learning (RL), a subfield of machine learning, learns to make optimal
decisions by interacting with the environment. RL agents maximize long-term payoffs
by mapping the current state to actions and continually optimizing the decision-making
strategy through a trial-and-error process [23]. In recent years, more and more studies have
combined reinforcement learning with metaheuristics to form a metaheuristic approach
for extracting knowledge from data, and this approach improves algorithm performance
by dynamically adjusting the decision-making strategy during the search process [24–26].

Due to the need for the sustainable development of smart cities, pure cleaning effi-

paper, we propose a multi-objective path planning model for unmanned sweepers (ETM-

shortcomings of traditional heuristic algorithms relying on experience and problems, this

heuristic operators. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
•
We propose a new multi-objective mixed-integer nonlinear planning model (ETM-
ARP) that aims to minimize the energy consumption (E) and travel time (T) of the
unmanned sweeper by taking into account factors such as traffic signals and work
mode switching.
•

selecting operator choices and parameter configurations in ALNS during the search
period, and design destruction and repair operators.
•
The proposed QALNS is embedded into non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II
(NSGA-II), and specialized reward functions and acceptance levels are designed for
the movement of Pareto frontier solutions to obtain higher diversity and quality. The
experimental results demonstrate that our algorithm performs superiorly in terms of
the computational efficiency, quality, and diversity of Pareto frontier solutions.

2. Mathematical Modeling
This section describes the ETM-ARP problem established in this paper. First, the
directed graph and routing-related constraints are defined. Then, a traffic light model of
the road network and a detailed energy consumption model of the unmanned sweeper are
presented, in which various constraints are considered, such as vehicle dynamics, potential
energy difference, and battery efficiency. Finally, a multi-objective path planning model
that allows the unmanned sweeper to minimize its energy consumption (E) and travel time
(T) is developed.
2.1. Problem Description
Consider a complete directed graph G = (V, E), where V denotes the set of n vertices
in the graph, and the arc set E = {e1, e2, · · · , em} denotes that there are m arc segments in
the graph, which includes the demand set Rw and the non-demand set Rn. The unmanned
sweeper vehicle sweeps the road network by traversing all the demand sets Rw to complete
the road sweeping work. The following assumptions are made in this paper:
•
The distance between locations is measured using the Euclidean distance.
•
The unmanned sweeper is fully charged at the start of the sweeping task and does not
need to be recharged during the sweeping route.
•
The unmanned sweeper has two modes, sweeping mode and cruising mode, corre-
sponding to different speeds, vclean and vcruise.
•
The energy consumption model of the unmanned sweeper is a nonlinear model that
takes into account the longitudinal dynamics of the vehicle.
•
The impact of other vehicles, non-motorized vehicles, and pedestrians on unmanned
sweepers is not taken into account.

3. Improved Multi-Objective Planning Algorithm Based on Reinforcement Learning
The model presented in Section 2 is a multi-objective mixed-integer programming
model. In multi-objective optimization problems (MOPs), a unique global optimal solution
typically does not exist. Instead, the optimization results are often represented in the form
of a Pareto optimal solution set, consisting of non-dominated solutions. This paper aims to
find the Pareto-optimal solution set of the proposed model in order to balance the trade-offs
among the objectives and provide multiple solutions for unmanned sweeper path planning.
In this section, we propose a multi-objective planning algorithm based on improved
deep reinforcement learning (QALNS-NSGA-II). Figure 1 shows an algorithmic framework
diagram of QALNS-NSGA-II. In Step 1, we employ a method combining random generation
and opposition-based learning (OBL) to produce initial feasible solutions. This approach,
which simultaneously considers initial guesses and their opposites, has been demonstrated
to perform well in [27]. In step 2, an elite preservation strategy is used for retaining the
optimal individuals [28]. This process utilizes fast non-dominated sorting and crowding
distance calculation. In step 3 a modified ALNS algorithm based on DQN (QALNS) is
used for generating new populations; then QALNS is utilized to drive NSGA-II until the
convergence criteria are met. Finally, the final obtained Pareto frontier solution is outputted
in step 4.

3.2. Removal and Repair Operator
When dealing with the initial solution and the solutions generated during subsequent
iterations, different operators need to be set up for neighborhood operations according
to specific optimization needs. These operators are divided into two groups: removal
operators and repair operators. The removal operator destroys the structure of the solution,
while the repair operator repairs the solution to obtain a new one. By reasonably combining
the operators and adjusting the parameters, the performance of the algorithm and the
solving ability can be effectively improved.

3.2.2. Repair Operator
Random Repair: During the neighborhood search, we randomly select N non-repeating
insertion positions and insert the removed objects into them.
Greedy Repair: During the neighborhood search, we insert the removed object in
front of the one that has a higher growth rate of additional energy consumption than the
currently removed objects.
Beam Repair: During the neighborhood search, all positions are traversed sequentially,
all removed objects are inserted uniformly, and the position with the smallest time is
selected for insertion.
3.3. RL-Based ALNS Strategy Selection
Due to the lack of effective knowledge accumulation, the traditional ALNS method
is prone to local optimization and slow convergence. To improve this deficiency, we
introduce deep reinforcement learning to drive ALNS through DQN in the iterative process
to dynamically select the most suitable operation operators and parameters, to enhance
the convergence and global search capability of the algorithm. In this section, the basic
elements of DQN-based ALNS strategy selection are defined, including search space, agent,
state, action, and reward. In this paper, each solution is considered as an agent, and the
search space can be regarded as the environment in which the agent is located. The actions,
states, and rewards of the agent are defined as follows.
3.3.1. State
In order to obtain high-quality and diverse Pareto frontier solutions, it is required to
design the state of reinforcement learning with representations of the features that react
to its performance during the search process, thus guiding the agent to choose the best
action (operator) during each iteration. Therefore, in this paper, we design the agent’s state
as follows:
Energycur and tcur denote the E and T of the current solution, respectively; Energymin
and tmin denote the minimum E and the minimum T, respectively; and iteration denotes
the current number of iterations. These five state characteristics enable the agent to know
roughly how it performs during the search period, and help the agent to select an effective
strategy persistently throughout the search process to assist at a macro level.
Energygap and tgap denote the difference in E and T between the current solution and
the optimal solution, respectively, and accept indicates whether the current solution is
selected or not. These states help the agent balance the diversity of Pareto frontier solutions
and the speed of convergence in the current step.
was_changed denotes whether the current solution is different from the previous one,
unseen indicates whether the current solution has been encountered during the search,
last_action indicates the operation selected in the previous step, and improved indicates
whether the current solution has been improved compared to the previous one. These
states provide information about the results of the agent’s operations in the previous step
and the current one, and help to avoid ineffective repetition of choices at a micro level.

4. Numerical Experiments
In this section, the performance of the proposed model and algorithm is evaluated in
different cases. First, performance indicators are defined to measure the performance of
the algorithms. Due to the lack of standardized benchmark test cases, the test cases are
generated according to certain rules. Then, the importance of including characteristics such
as dynamic changes in on-board mass and slope in the energy function is evaluated. Third,
the QALNS-NSGA-II method proposed in this paper is compared with ALNS-NSGA-II
and classical NSGA-II. Finally, sensitivity analysis experiments are performed.
The numerical experiments used PyCharm Community 2023.3 on a PC with a 12th

To emphasize the importance of including dynamic loads and road slope in the energy
consumption model, we consider two different cases designed for comparative analysis
using the road network of Sioux Falls, which consists of 24 nodes and 76 arcs. In the first

preferences from the Pareto solution set.

two stages: the rapid descent phase and the slow descent phase. In the rapid descent phase,
T decreases quickly with a slight increase in E. This is because the unmanned sweepers
effectively avoid the red light period of the traffic signal by reasonably planning the path
and quickly adjusting the timing of passing through the heavy-duty section in this phase,
thus reducing the waiting time. The strategy in this phase tends to sacrifice a small amount

5. Conclusions
In this paper, we study an unmanned sweeper arc route planning problem with both
efficiency- and energy-saving-related objectives (travel time T and energy consumption E)
and establish a new multi-objective mixed-integer nonlinear planning model, which takes
into account the effects of road slope, dynamic changes in on-board mass, mode switching
of vehicle operation, and traffic lights, which makes the model more realistic.
To solve the problem, we design encoding and decoding methods for the arc path planning
problem and repair and destruction operators for the adaptive large-scale neighborhood search
algorithm based on the features of the problem. In addition, a selected hyperheuristic based
on deep reinforcement learning (QALNS) is designed to adaptively select operators during the
iteration process using DQN-driven ALNS, and a specialized reward function is designed for the
process of Pareto frontier solution iteration to drive accelerated convergence.
QALNS is embedded into non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II) to
form a new algorithm, QALNS-NSGA-II. We conducted a comprehensive set of experiments
to evaluate the performance of our proposed algorithm. We compared QALNS-NSGA-II
with the classical NSGA-II and ALNS-NSGA-II. The experimental results show that the
proposed algorithm has a remarkable advantage for this problem, both in terms of the
speed of exploring Pareto frontier solutions and the quality (diversity and convergence) of
the explored Pareto frontier solutions.
In addition, some planning insights can be gained from the experimental analysis. As
E increases, T first declines rapidly followed by a gentle decline. The planner can trade-off
between these two objectives to find a compromise point suitable for practical needs. In
addition, according to the experimental results of unmanned sweeper path planning, the
sweeping mode of “light load first, heavy load later” can effectively reduce the unnecessary
waste of resources. Under the condition that the total path length remains unchanged, this
mode significantly reduces the total trip energy consumption of the unmanned sweeper.
Therefore, it is recommended that the initial position of the unmanned sweeper be assigned
to neighborhoods with less garbage distribution, to optimize whole path planning and
further improve energy utilization efficiency.
The limitations of this study and future research directions are summarized as follows: In
this paper, a reinforcement learning integrated metaheuristic algorithm is used to implement
adaptive operator selection, and a random initial population and fixed parameters are used in
the training process. However, this approach limits the convergence speed and optimization
effect of the algorithm to some extent, which may affect its applicability to complex problems. To
address this limitation, future research can further optimize the population initialization strategy
and explore the dynamic parameter adjustment method to improve the search efficiency and
global convergence ability of the algorithm. In addition, future research should also thoroughly
analyze the effects of dynamics and stochasticity on path planning in real-world scenarios,
combine the advantages of reinforcement learning in high-dimensional data processing, and
develop more robust and efficient path planning algorithms for unmanned sweeper vehicles, so
as to better meet the needs of practical applications.
